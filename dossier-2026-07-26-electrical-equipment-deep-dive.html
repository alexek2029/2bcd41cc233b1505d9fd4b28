00 · Executive verdict

Six names survive the funnel; two lead it

The one-paragraph read

Twenty-one names went in. The valuation, health, catalyst and timing gates cut them to six: ETN, HUBB, VRT, ENS, NXT, CARR — with Eaton and Hubbell the highest-probability first entries, Vertiv the higher-octane alternative.

The sector evidence got stronger one level down: Electrical Equipment reports growth on every single ISM sub-index — orders, production, employment, backlogs, and prices rising while supplier deliveries slow — the textbook signature of demand outrunning supply ENGINE. The funnel then did its job of separating the theme from the tradeable names: the biggest "bargain" (First Solar at 8.7× forward) and four others fell to Anton's tumbleweed rule — too few catalysts between earnings to move in a 20–60 day window — and two adjacents (Emerson, Johnson Controls) failed the health screen. Entries before those prints are event bets, not process — the process says let the prints land, then take the timing engine's entry.

01 · The top-down hand-off

Why we are digging here, and what "long bias" obliges us to do

Dossier 01 (26 Jul) set a +10–20% net-long tilt and made L1 — Electrical equipment & grid infrastructure its most engine-corroborated long bucket: ISM #2 growth industry on a 5-month streak, new orders 56.0 outrunning inventories (ratio 1.09), the rates engine explicitly long short-duration industrials, and copper's abnormal +37.8% YoY confirming an electrification build-out independently ENGINE.

The method for going one level down is prescribed, not improvised. The ISM gives the predisposition and points at industries; it never gives the trade — "you don't just look at this in the ISM and then decide to put a trade on… that's a rookie error. You've got to go through all of the other processes" ANTON · PTM 2.0 L7 1:31:10. Those processes, in order, are the funnel this dossier runs ANTON · PTM 2.0 L22–27 (quant), L30–32 (qual/catalysts), L37–38 (timing):

  1. Define the peer set — every qualifying name in the industry, mid-cap and up for longs (§03).
  2. Valuation outlier screen — forward P/E against the peer median, with earnings growth (§04).
  3. Balance-sheet health — long GOOD fundamentals only (§05).
  4. Catalyst frequency — does the name generate news between earnings, or is it a tumbleweed? (§06).
  5. Technical timing — trend, support/resistance, the entry read (§07), with volatility context (§08).

Looking for longs here means the screen wants the double discount: cheaper than peers on forward earnings while growing earnings and carrying a clean balance sheet — quality the market is underpricing, inside an industry the ISM says is accelerating. Expensive names aren't disqualified (momentum is real), but they must earn their multiple through the catalyst and timing gates.

Glossary for this section
Top-down / bottom-up
Top-down: environment → sector (Dossier 01). Bottom-up: individual company evidence (this dossier). Anton's tree runs GDP → Market → Sector → Stocks — "go where the juice is".
Predisposition
The directional lean the macro data gives you before you look at any stock — here, long this industry. It biases the search; it never places the trade.
Peer set
The full list of comparable companies in the same industry. Valuation only means something relative to peers — a P/E is cheap or expensive against the set's median, not in a vacuum.
Funnel
A sequence of elimination gates. Names must pass every gate; each gate can only remove, never re-admit. 21 in, 6 out is the funnel working.
Double discount
Our shorthand for the long profile the screen hunts: priced below peers and growing faster than the price implies, with health ruling out the cheap-for-a-reason trap.

Glossary for this section
Sub-indices
The ISM headline number is built from component questions — new orders, production, employment, supplier deliveries, inventories. Each is its own diffusion index; new orders is the most forward-looking of them.
Order backlog
Work contracted but not yet delivered. A growing backlog means future revenue is already booked — the "order-book-visible earnings" our rate regime favours.
Supplier deliveries (why "slowing" is growth)
This index rises when deliveries get slower — because in a survey of buyers, slow deliveries mean suppliers are swamped. It's the one index where worse service = stronger demand signal.
Pricing power
The ability to raise prices without losing the sale — visible here as the industry topping the "prices rising" list while orders and backlogs still grow. Protects margins even if input costs rise.
Respondent comments
Verbatim quotes from surveyed purchasing managers, published with each ISM report — the qualitative layer under the numbers.

Glossary for this section
Forward vs trailing P/E
Trailing uses the last 12 months' reported earnings; forward uses the next 12 months' forecast. A big gap between them (Vertiv: 73× trailing → 33× forward) is the market expecting earnings to roughly double.
Median (vs average)
The middle value of the pool — half the names above, half below. Used instead of the average so one extreme name can't drag the benchmark.
EG1 (earnings growth, year 1)
The percentage change from last year's earnings per share to this year's forecast. Anton pairs it with the P/E: cheap + growing is the outlier you want; cheap + shrinking is a value trap.
EV/EBITDA
Enterprise value (market cap + debt − cash) over cash operating profits. A second valuation lens that can't be flattered by debt or tax quirks — used here to confirm the P/E story.
Value trap
A stock that looks cheap because the market correctly expects its earnings to fall. The health gate (§05) and growth column exist to filter these out.
n/m (not meaningful)
A ratio whose inputs make it useless — e.g. dividing by near-zero depressed earnings produces a huge number that says nothing.

Glossary for this section
Debt-to-equity (D/E)
Total debt divided by shareholders' equity. Roughly: how much of the company is built on borrowed money. 0.72 = 72p of debt per £1 of equity — moderate.
Interest coverage
Operating profit ÷ interest bill. 18.5× means profits cover the interest 18 times over. Anton's benchmark: 3.33× is the line associated with investment-grade credit; below ~1.5× a company is working for its lenders.
Current ratio
Short-term assets ÷ short-term liabilities. Below 1 means the company can't cover the next year's bills from the next year's assets — the insolvency warning line.
Working capital / total assets
The cash cushion in day-to-day operations, scaled by company size. Persistent negative readings flag operational strain.
ROE / net margin / FCF yield
Return on equity (profit per £1 of shareholder capital), profit kept per £1 of sales, and free cash generated per £1 of market value — the quality trio: is the business actually good, and does the profit turn into cash?

Glossary for this section
Catalyst
An event that forces the market to reprice a stock — earnings, contract wins, guidance changes, product launches. No catalyst, no move; no move, no 20–60 day trade.
8-K filing
The SEC form US companies must file within days of any material event. Counting non-earnings 8-Ks measures how often a company generates real news between quarters — an objective, unfakeable activity meter.
Tumbleweed
Anton's term for a stock with almost no news flow between earnings reports. It can be cheap and healthy and still dead money for two months — which is why it's cut regardless of valuation.
Known vs unknown catalysts
Earnings dates are known — everyone has them diarised, and the price carries the consensus expectation. The between-earnings flow is where a position gets paid without the whole market braced for the event.

Glossary for this section
Moving average (MA)
The average price over the last N days, redrawn daily — a smoothed trend line. Anton's checklist uses the 20/60/120/250-day set; price above a rising MA = uptrend evidence. "Fast" = shorter window, "slow" = longer.
Support / resistance
Price levels where buying (support) or selling (resistance) has repeatedly appeared. Entries near support with room to resistance give a favourable ratio of upside room to stop distance.
Higher-highs / higher-lows
The definition of an uptrend in price structure: each rally peaks higher and each dip bottoms higher than the last.
Range
Price oscillating between support and resistance with no trend. Playable (buy low end, sell high end) but different from trend-following.
Breakout
Price clearing resistance decisively — often the trigger that converts a coiled range into a new trend leg (the HUBB and CARR setups).
Binary event
A scheduled moment (earnings, FOMC) where the price gaps on the outcome. Holding through one is a coin-flip exposure, not a process edge.

Glossary for this section
ATRP (Average True Range %)
The average size of a bar's full trading range (high to low, gap-adjusted), as a percentage of price. Monthly ATRP ≈ how far the stock typically travels in a month. It measures movement, not direction.
DoR (Distribution of Returns)
The statistical fingerprint of a stock's returns — mean, spread (standard deviation), and how fat the tails are. The course's first lens on any instrument.
Why we don't screen on volatility
Movement is the raw material of returns — you want lively names. The risk constraint (15–30% annualised) binds the portfolio, where sizing and diversification bring hot names down to a controlled total.
Sizing
Choosing position value so each idea contributes comparable risk: hotter name, smaller position. The last deterministic step before any order.
