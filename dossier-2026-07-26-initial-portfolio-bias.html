03 · The forward read

What the next ~6 months look like from here

The leading set leads GDP — and equities discount GDP with a roughly two-quarter lead, the taught structure behind the whole top-down method (peak correlation with the S&P 500 at a ~6-month lead) ANTON · PTM 2.0 L3 18:40, 39:05 ENGINE · macro_gdp. Weighing every signal's direction of travel, not just its level:

The central case (we estimate ~60%): expansion continues into Q4-26/Q1-27, but gets choppier and narrower. The evidence for continuation is broad and independent: new orders at 56 with a positive orders-to-inventories ratio (1.09) says the manufacturing pipeline is still filling; services backlogs are rising; M2 (93rd percentile) and Fed net liquidity (82nd percentile, +$105bn on the week) are both expanding — the liquidity impulse leads risk assets; China's credit-sensitive Caixin survey is at an 86th-percentile reading and rising; and copper — the most GDP-sensitive price on the board — is up an abnormal +37.8% YoY. Payrolls and claims confirm the present. That is not a picture that typically rolls into recession within two quarters. ENGINE

The risk case (~30%): a rate-led compression of multiples that hits the index without hitting the economy. The real 10y has risen 51bp in six months into the 96th percentile while breakevens sit still — a pure discount-rate move. If it continues (supply pressure, a hawkish hold on 29 July, or the 10.1% YoY PPI pipeline forcing term-premium higher), the equity damage concentrates in long-duration growth — precisely the crowded end of the AI trade — while short-duration cyclical earnings are comparatively insulated. The CCC−B extreme and the one-day HY widening flagged by the intake layer are consistent with markets beginning to price this. Note this scenario redistributes returns rather than destroying them: it is why our longs are cash-flow-now sectors and our shorts are multiple-dependent ones — the book is designed to make money in both the central and the risk case.

The left tail (~10%): the consumer crack spreads. Sentiment at 44.8 is literally the 0th five-year percentile — below the historical trough zone — and the consumer is two-thirds of the economy ANTON · UMCSI framework, PTM 2.0 leading-indicator block. Today it is contradicted by the hard data (retail trade growing in the ISM services ranking, accommodation & food on a 10-month growth streak, claims falling). Two honest readings coexist: it is either the single best forward warning on the board, or — at a 0th-percentile extreme — a contrarian trough with the expectations sub-index already bouncing (+6.6 on the month). The falling 13-week trend keeps its vote OFF; the bounce in expectations is what we watch for the turn. Sentiment troughs are where you watch for the turn, not where you initiate shorts against a confirming real economy.

07 · Deep dive

The AI vertical: where the value filters down

The question posed: with the purest plays priced for perfection, where in the vertical does the money funnel next? The hypothesis is yours; the method is Anton's (theme → sector → stock, confirmed by objective data); the tier mapping is the agent's reasoning, stated in the open. The answer, in one line: value migrates from the layer with infinite multiple to the layer with finite supply.

What the objective data already shows

We don't need the financial press to test this thesis — the deterministic engines are already measuring it, in three independent places. One: the ISM manufacturing panel ranks Electrical Equipment, Appliances & Components #2 of all US manufacturing industries (▲3, 5-month growth streak) and Computer & Electronic Products #7 (8-month streak) — purchasing managers reporting real orders, in a survey where new orders (56.0) outrun production (52.2) and inventories (ratio 1.09). An electrification-scale investment cycle is landing in the order books of the equipment layer. Two: copper — the raw material of that build-out — is +37.8% YoY, the only abnormal print in the commodity basket (z = +1.48). Three: the SOXX/SPY internal reads Neutral: semiconductor volumes are expanding (per the ISM) while semiconductor relative price leadership has stalled ENGINE · all three. Expanding units plus flat relative price at the top of the stack, while the layers beneath climb the ISM rankings, is precisely the signature of value filtering down the vertical. The rates engine supplies the accelerant: a 96th-percentile real yield punishes the infinite-duration multiple and leaves order-book earnings alone.

The forward logic, justified. Every dollar of Tier-0/1 investment becomes revenue at Tiers 2–4 regardless of which model or chip vendor wins the race — the spend flows down the vertical mechanically. That makes the lower tiers a lower-variance claim on the same theme: they monetise the build-out, not the outcome of the model race — and the ISM rankings show that flow is already arriving in their order books. 08 · Deep dive

Gold miners: right idea, wrong trigger — for now

Watchlist · Not a macro long today

The honest verdict

The corpus check first. Retrieval against the ingested course tops out at 0.545 similarity for a gold-vs-macro rule — below the 0.55 grounding gate — so there is no Anton-cited rule linking gold to the macro cycle, and this dossier will not invent one. What the cited framework does supply is the discount-rate lens: real rates, not nominal, are what matter and "stand the test of time" in the framework ANTON · PTM 2.0 L4 40:17.

What our measured data says. Gold's macro case is, mechanically, a falling-real-yield case: a zero-yield asset competes with the real risk-free return. Our tape says that case has not begun — the real 10y is at 2.43%, the 96th five-year percentile, +25bp in four weeks, +51bp in six months, still rising ENGINE. A 2.4% real risk-free return is direct competition for a zero-yield asset. Note also that the commodity engine deliberately excludes gold from its cyclical basket — it is a monetary asset, not a growth commodity — so this month's copper strength says nothing in gold's favour. 12 · Glossary

Plain-English glossary

Every piece of jargon in this dossier, explained for a smart reader who doesn't live in a dealing room. Terms are grouped the way the dossier uses them.

Duration, multiples & the discount rate — the core idea of this book

Duration (of a stock)
How far in the future the bulk of a company's profits sit. A machinery maker paid on this year's order book earns its money now — short duration. A growth story whose big profits are promised in 2032 is long duration. The word is borrowed from bonds, where it measures the same thing: how long you wait to get paid.
Discount rate
The interest rate used to translate future money into today's money. £100 arriving in 10 years is worth much less today if you could instead earn 2.4% above inflation risk-free — which is exactly what the real 10-year Treasury yield offers right now. The real yield is the market's base discount rate for everything.
Short-duration cyclical earnings (our longs)
Profits that arrive soon (visible order books, this year's cash flow) from businesses tied to the economic cycle — electrical equipment, metals, banks, freight, energy. Two reasons we want them now: (1) when the discount rate rises, near-term profits lose almost no present value, so these stocks are insulated from the rate move; (2) the leading indicators say the cycle is expanding, which is precisely when these businesses' earnings grow. Both tailwinds, one bucket.
Multiple (P/E ratio)
The price you pay per £1 of a company's annual earnings. A stock on a 40× multiple costs £40 per £1 of current profit — you're paying up today for growth promised later. "High-multiple" and "long-duration" usually describe the same stocks.
"Short long-duration multiples" (our shorts)
Betting against expensive stocks whose value depends on distant future profits. When the real yield rises, those distant profits are discounted more heavily, so the fair multiple mathematically shrinks — the share price can fall even if the business performs fine. This is the mirror image of the longs above: one rate move, two trades.
De-rating / multiple compression
A share price falling because the multiple shrinks, not because earnings fell. The main way rising rates hurt expensive stocks.
Bond proxy
A stable, dividend-paying stock (classically a regulated utility) that investors own as a bond substitute. When actual bond yields rise, the dividend looks less special and the stock de-rates — which is the S3 short.

Running the book

Long / short
Long = own it, profit if it rises. Short = borrow and sell it, profit if it falls (buy it back cheaper later).
Long/Short absolute-return book
A portfolio holding both longs and shorts, aiming to make money whether the market rises or falls — judged on its own return, not on beating an index.
Net tilt / net exposure
Longs minus shorts, as a share of the book. +15% net long means the long side is modestly bigger — we lean long without depending on the market rising. Gross exposure is longs plus shorts: how much capital is deployed in total.
Bucket
One independent idea/theme. We take one stock per bucket so that no two positions are secretly the same bet — diversification comes from ideas that don't move together, not from owning more tickers.
Correlation
How much two positions move together. Ten highly correlated stocks are one big bet in disguise; that's why the buckets matter more than the count.
Conviction (probability-weighted)
How strongly we hold a view — scaled to the number of independent signals agreeing, never certainty. Every view here has a stated probability and a stated way to be proven wrong.
Invalidation
The specific, pre-agreed evidence that would prove an idea wrong — written down before entering, so we exit on evidence instead of rationalising losses.
Watchlist / trigger
An idea we like but won't touch until a defined condition fires (e.g. gold miners wait for real-yield momentum to turn negative).
Reward-to-risk (1:3)
Position targets must offer at least £3 of gain per £1 risked to the stop-loss.
Paper portfolio
Simulated money, real process. No live orders; the discipline is the point.

Rates & inflation

Nominal vs real yield
Nominal = the stated interest rate. Real = the rate after expected inflation (measured from inflation-protected Treasuries, TIPS). Real is what matters for valuing assets — it's the true reward for waiting.
Breakeven (10y)
The market's implied inflation expectation: nominal yield minus real yield (4.71% − 2.43% ≈ 2.26%). Ours being flat while yields rise tells us the move is a real-rate move, not an inflation scare.
2s10s curve
The 10-year Treasury yield minus the 2-year. Positive and steepening usually signals growth expectations; an inverted curve (negative) has historically preceded recessions. Ours: +36bp, positive.
Bear steepener
The curve steepening because long yields are rising (falling bond prices = "bear" for bonds). Good for bank margins, bad for long-duration stocks — both are in this book.
Basis point (bp)
One hundredth of a percent. +25bp = +0.25%.
FOMC
The Federal Reserve committee that sets US interest rates. Its meetings (next: 29 July) can reprice the entire rates picture in an afternoon.
CPI / PPI
Consumer vs producer (factory-gate) inflation. PPI tends to lead CPI — a hot PPI (10.1%) means pipeline pressure that may keep rates rising.
M2 / net liquidity
M2 = the broad money supply. Net liquidity = the Fed's balance sheet minus the Treasury's cash account (TGA) minus money parked back at the Fed (reverse repo) — a measure of cash actually available to markets. Both rising = fuel for asset prices.

Credit — the early-warning system

Credit spread / HY OAS
The extra yield "high-yield" (junk-rated) borrowers must pay over Treasuries. Widening spreads = the market pricing more default risk = stress. Ours is tight (calm) at the 15th percentile.
CCC−B decompression
The gap between the riskiest junk (CCC-rated) and moderately risky junk (B-rated). When this gap blows out while headline spreads stay calm — exactly today's picture, 99th percentile — the market is quietly repricing its weakest borrowers first. "The junk moves first" is the classic early crack.

Volatility & market internals

VIX
The market's expected volatility for the S&P 500 over the next month, implied by option prices — the "fear gauge". Our traffic-light monitor sits on top of it: CALM means build/hold the book.
VIX3M/VIX
Three-month vs one-month expected volatility. Normally 3-month is higher; when the near month rises relative to it, markets are bracing for near-term trouble — currently one of our two bearish internal divergences.
MOVE
The bond market's version of the VIX. Bond volatility often leads equity volatility; ours is calm (69.6).
Relative-strength ratio (e.g. SOXX/SPY)
A sector ETF's price divided by the market's. Rising = the sector is leading; flat/falling = leadership stalling. A bearish divergence is when the market makes highs but the ratio doesn't confirm them.
Breadth (% above 200-day average)
The share of all stocks trading above their long-term trend line. Healthy markets rise on broad participation (ours: 66%), not a handful of giants.
McClellan oscillator
A momentum gauge built from daily advancing-vs-declining stock counts; near zero (ours: −4) means neither buying nor selling pressure dominates.

The macro signals

Leading vs coincident indicators
Leading indicators (ISM surveys, permits, sentiment, the curve) move ~6 months before the economy; they set the view. Coincident indicators (payrolls, claims, industrial production) describe now; we use them only to confirm, never to initiate.
ISM PMI (manufacturing / services)
Monthly surveys of purchasing managers — the people who see orders first. Above 50 = expansion, below = contraction. The sub-indices matter (new orders lead everything), and the 18-industry rankings tell us which industries are growing — that's where the sector ideas come from.
Orders-to-inventories ratio
New orders divided by inventories. Above 1 (ours: 1.09) means demand is outrunning stock — production has to catch up. A forward signal inside a forward signal.
Growth breadth (ISM)
How many of the 18 surveyed industries report growth vs contraction (14/3 this month, from 16/0). Narrowing breadth = the expansion resting on fewer legs — a crack worth watching even while the headline is strong.
Building permits
Government approvals to start building homes — spending committed before the economy feels it. Leads GDP by 8–16 months; the housing chain shorts (S1) key off it.
UMCSI
University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index — how households feel. The consumer is ~two-thirds of the US economy; ours is at a 5-year low (44.8), the loudest bearish signal on the board.
NFIB
The small-business optimism survey — small firms employ roughly half of America. Decent zone, forward components improving.
Caixin vs official PMI (China)
Two Chinese manufacturing surveys: the state's official one (big state firms) and Caixin's private one (smaller exporters). We watch both; when they agree on expansion (now), the read is more trustworthy.
ESI (Europe)
The EU's Economic Sentiment Indicator, 100 = long-run average. At 95 and falling, Europe is the soft spot in the global backdrop.
Percentile (5-year)
Where today's value sits against the last five years of readings. 96th percentile = higher than 96% of all readings in that window — i.e. near the extreme.
Z-score
How far a reading is from its own average, measured in standard deviations. Beyond about ±1.5 we call it abnormal — copper's +1.48 is right at that line.
Confluence
The count of independent signals pointing the same way (15 risk-on / 3 risk-off / 2 neutral). Conviction scales with confluence — one signal is an anecdote, fifteen is an environment.
