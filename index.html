The reading path · leading → coincident
01

Consumer Sentiment

◆ Zonal read: level maps to GDP/S&P; Expectations index leads

Because consumer spending is ~67% of the US economy, the University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index is a powerful leading indicator of GDP growth, S&P 500 direction and Fed policy.
02

Building Permits: The Housing Leading Indicator

◆ Future US GDP growth, S&P 500 direction, and credit-market health.

A building permit is the paperwork stage before a home is built, so tracking new permits gives an early read on GDP growth, S&P 500 direction, and the health of the US credit market.
03

NFIB Small Business Optimism Index

◆ Above 100 bullish; below 95 warns of slowdown

The NFIB Small Business Optimism Index tracks the ~50% of the US economy that small business represents, and a bespoke leading sub-index of its forward-looking components signals GDP and S&P 500 direction ahead of the headline.
04

Cyclical Commodities as a Leading Indicator

◆ Demand-led cyclical commodity prices lead future inflation and GDP.

Cyclical commodities trade every day and move with the business cycle, so their prices lead the monthly inflation numbers (CPI/PPI) and quarterly Real GDP, which are only reported after the fact.
05

European Economic Sentiment Indicator (ESI)

◆ ESI >100 = above-average Eurozone sentiment; focus EA + big 3

The ESI wraps all of Europe's leading indicator surveys into one timely monthly composite (scaled to a mean of 100), which you read to forecast Eurozone GDP, Eurostoxx 600 returns and your portfolio bias.
06

China Leading Indicators: Official vs Caixin PMI and Real Rates

◆ China: trust Caixin over official PMI; trade via US ADRs.

Because Chinese GDP stats are manipulated and the indices decouple from growth, use the Caixin PMI, its divergence from the official PMI, and China real rates to read China and trade it through US-listed ADRs.
07

Recap: Bringing the Leading Indicators Together

◆ Leading indicators forecast; coincident confirm; lagging report last.

Leading indicators (money markets, surveys, commodities, stock/FX prices) forecast the economy; coincident indicators confirm or deny that forecast, and lagging indicators report it last — so we position 20-60 days ahead of the reports, not on them.
